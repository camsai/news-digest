      Data-driven materials development requires the collection of large amounts
      of high-quality materials data. Full autonomy of materials experiments is
      anticipated, but its technical hurdles are high and its adoption remains
      limited. In this study, we constructed a simple, easy-to-deploy automated
      experimentation platform that focuses not on full autonomy but on the
      reliable automation and quantitative recording of experimental processes.
      Specifically, commercially available instruments such as robot arms,
      electric pipettes, web cameras, and an electronic balance are combined,
      components such as fixtures are fabricated with a 3D printer, and the
      instruments are operated by control code generated by an AI agent based on
      a large language model. As a demonstration, we applied the platform to a
      two-solution mixing experimental system and synthesized ZIF-8, a
      metal-organic framework. A white suspension phase was observed in the
      product, and X-ray diffraction measurements confirmed that it was ZIF-8.
      We also found that its particle size distribution depends strongly on the
      solution dispensing speed of the electric pipette, which is a parameter
      that is difficult to control or record in manual operation. This
      dependence was reproduced in repeated runs, confirming the repeatability
      of the automated synthesis. This result is a good example showing that the
      control and recording of process parameters that are rarely quantified in
      manual work can govern the quality of materials data. All control code,
      CAD models, and documentation are made publicly available to encourage the
      spread of laboratory-scale automation of experiments.

      Benchmarking Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials for Dynamical
      Stability in Inorganic Semiconductor Nanocrystals: A CdSe Case Study
    url: "https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15299v1"
    publishedAt: "2026-09-14"
    type: Preprint
    access: Abstract only
    evidence: >-
      Machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) enable nanosecond-scale
      atomistic simulations of inorganic semiconductor nanocrystals, but low
      errors on held-out configurations do not necessarily guarantee stable
      molecular dynamics. We benchmark five graph-neural-network MLIPs, SchNet,
      PaiNN, NequIP, Allegro and MACE, for dynamical stability in a
      chloride-passivated cadmium selenide nanocluster containing 149 atoms. The
      models were trained under harmonized conditions on 1,000 configurations
      and evaluated against 2,000 held-out configurations generated using
      density functional theory, considering force accuracy, computational
      efficiency, uncertainty and structural stability during 1 ns simulations
      at 300 K. Allegro produced the lowest validation force mean absolute
      errors, ranging from 12 to 14 meV per angstrom, whereas SchNet produced
      the largest, ranging from 84 to 110 meV per angstrom. This ranking did not
      predict dynamical robustness: NequIP remained stable for 1 ns without
      additional training data, whereas MACE became stable only after an
      ensemble-based active-learning procedure added 34 uncertainty-selected
      configurations. PaiNN and Allegro remained unstable after the addition of
      100 and 95 configurations, respectively, and SchNet also failed to achieve
      stable dynamics within the tested augmentation budget. Under the benchmark
      hardware conditions, the machine-learning potentials required 6-66 ms per
      molecular-dynamics step, compared with approximately 20 s for density
      functional theory. These results show that held-out prediction errors
      alone are insufficient for selecting interatomic potentials for finite,
      surface-dominated nanostructures. Reliable deployment requires
      long-timescale dynamical testing combined with uncertainty-guided
      refinement, while the effectiveness and data efficiency of active learning
      remain strongly architecture dependent.

  - title: Neural-Network Solutions to Real-Space Charge Density and Generalization
    url: "https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14906v1"
    publishedAt: "2026-09-14"
    type: Preprint
    access: Abstract only
    evidence: >-
      The Hohenberg-Kohn theorem establishes that, in principle, the ground
      state (GS) charge density contains all GS information of a many-electron
      system, such that all GS observables can be expressed as functionals of
      the GS charge density. Conventional Kohn-Sham density functional theory
      requires iterative solution of the self-consistent-field equations at
      substantial computational cost, motivating the development of deep
      learning surrogates for electronic structure calculations and, in turn,
      accelerating computer-aided materials design. Here, we propose
      \textbf{AIDEN}, an \underline{A}tomic-\underline{I}nteraction
      \underline{D}ensity \underline{E}quivariant \underline{N}etwork for
      solving real-space charge density. AIDEN separates the element-dependent
      one-center density from environment-induced density redistribution and
      represents the latter through complementary atom- and edge-centered tensor
      correlations. A continuous low-rank Gaussian decoder then reconstructs the
      density at arbitrary spatial coordinates while reusing atomic encodings
      independently of the evaluation grid. AIDEN achieves state-of-the-art
      accuracy on periodic crystal benchmarks while remaining competitive for
      molecular systems, and further demonstrates zero-shot transferability
      across several structurally distinct out-of-distribution case studies.
      Furthermore, AIDEN provides substantially faster inference than both
      baseline models and full SCF calculations, enabling efficient charge
      density reconstruction for large-scale electronic structure calculations.

## Featured: Open-source RHEED toolkit exposes measurements and analysis to AI agents

Researchers built an open-source hardware/software toolkit that automates reflection high-energy electron diffraction (RHEED) alignment, rocking-curve acquisition, and quantitative structural analysis during pulsed laser deposition. A Model Context Protocol server lets AI agents interpret natural-language requests to select and run analyses, with an extensible adapter interface for community-developed RHEED simulation and AI-analysis methods.

**Why it matters:** RHEED is one of the richest real-time probes of surface evolution during epitaxial growth, but manual operation has long been a bottleneck to closing the loop on autonomous synthesis. Making RHEED control and interpretation machine-addressable is a concrete step toward agent-directed thin-film deposition, with potential applications in automated materials research.

**Limitations:** This is a preprint demonstrating toolkit capability and a single agent-driven use case on one pulsed laser deposition system; the abstract does not establish closed-loop optimization of film quality or generalization across deposition techniques and materials classes.

## AI-generated control code supports reproducible MOF synthesis

**Why it matters:** Rather than chasing full autonomy, this work targets reliable, quantitative recording of process parameters that are typically invisible in manual lab work, addressing a key bottleneck for data-driven materials development: generating high-quality, reproducible synthesis data at scale using commercially available components.

**Limitations:** Demonstrated on a single well-characterized system (ZIF-8 precipitation); broader applicability to more complex, multi-step syntheses and to materials with less forgiving reaction windows is not established by the abstract.

## Benchmark shows validation accuracy doesn't predict MD stability for learned potentials

Comparing five graph neural network interatomic potentials (SchNet, PaiNN, NequIP, Allegro, MACE) on a chloride-passivated CdSe nanocluster, Allegro achieved the lowest force errors but this did not translate to stable molecular dynamics. NequIP ran stably for a full nanosecond without extra data, while MACE needed active-learning augmentation to stabilize, and PaiNN and Allegro remained unstable after adding 100 and 95 configurations, respectively. SchNet also failed within its tested augmentation budget.

**Why it matters:** This directly challenges the common practice of selecting machine-learning interatomic potentials purely by held-out force/energy error. For finite, surface-dominated nanostructures relevant to quantum dots and catalysts, long-timescale dynamical testing and uncertainty-guided active learning appear essential for trustworthy deployment.

**Limitations:** Findings are based on a single 149-atom CdSe nanocluster system and one thermodynamic condition (300 K, 1 ns); generalization to other nanocrystal chemistries, larger systems, or longer timescales is not established.

[Benchmarking Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials for Dynamical Stability in Inorganic Semiconductor Nanocrystals: A CdSe Case Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15299v1) — Preprint; Abstract only.

## Large SCF benchmark shows learned initializers don't always speed up DFT convergence

Using a new 43,851-crystal dataset (ρHD-43K) with aligned charge-density, Hamiltonian, and density-matrix labels, researchers tested whether learned predictions actually accelerate the self-consistent-field (SCF) loop of DFT, rather than just minimizing offline prediction error. Direct charge-density prediction accelerated about 91% of test crystals with a 1.18x SCF-loop speedup, but tested residual-density and Hamiltonian/matrix initializers did not consistently beat the standard atomic-density-superposition baseline.

**Why it matters:** This is a useful reality check for the growing field of ML-accelerated DFT: it shows that a learned model's compatibility with the nonlinear SCF solver trajectory, not just its target-space accuracy, determines real-world usefulness, which should reshape how such models are evaluated going forward.

**Limitations:** Benchmark is restricted to non-magnetic crystals on a frozen test set; extrapolation beyond the reported test conditions is not justified by the abstract, and the modest observed speedups (median 3 fewer SCF iterations) may not justify added model complexity in all workflows.

[Evaluating Predicted Densities, Hamiltonians, and Density Matrices as Periodic SCF Initializers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15151v1) — Preprint; Abstract only.

## Physics-guided ML models prescreen point defects in SiC for quantum applications

Ridge, kernel ridge, and multilayer perceptron models were trained to predict defect formation energies and zero-phonon lines for point defects in 4H-SiC without requiring costly structural relaxation. For vacancies and substitutions, mean absolute errors reached 0.437 eV (formation energy) and 0.202 eV (zero-phonon line), described as useful even beyond prescreening in some cases; interstitial defects showed larger errors (1.101 eV and 0.230 eV) still deemed useful for prescreening.

**Why it matters:** High-throughput DFT defect screening for quantum and power-electronics applications is computationally expensive; models that skip the relaxation step could substantially widen the search space explored before committing to costly first-principles calculations.

**Limitations:** Error levels for interstitial defects are explicitly described as insufficient for detailed characterization, and the abstract reports validation on 4H-SiC; it does not establish transferability to other semiconductor hosts or defect chemistries.

[Prescreening Point Defects in Semiconductors With Machine Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14846v1) — Preprint; Abstract only.
