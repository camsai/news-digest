    type: Preprint

    evidence: >-
      Atomistic foundation models have emerged as a promising route to DFT-level
      accuracy for materials simulation with complex chemical environments.
      However, their reliability across competing bonding regimes and, more
      importantly, how complex bonding affects predictions of material
      properties remain unclear. Among materials with competing bonding regimes,
      sodium storage in hard carbon poses a substantial challenge. Hard carbon
      features diverse local structural motifs, including defective and curved
      graphitic regions, amorphous regions, and closed/open pores. Motivated by
      its importance for Na-ion batteries, we systematically evaluate existing
      pretrained foundation models based on the MACE architecture across
      different local Na/C motifs. We find that the pretrained models fail to
      accurately capture Na-Na and Na-C interactions for metallic Na clusters on
      graphene. To recover a DFT-level energetic description, we employ targeted
      fine-tuning, showing that it can describe the energetic stability of local
      motifs and achieve sufficient transferability to unseen Na/C environments.
      With the developed fine-tuned model, we establish a correlation between
      closed-pore radius in hard carbon and plateau voltage, showing that the
      small pores contribute at larger voltages than large ones, suggesting thus
      how the developed fine-tuned foundation model can link atomistic
      information with materials design for complex bonding environments.
  - title: >-
      Diffusion Tree Sampling for Inference Time Adaptation of Crystal
      Generative Models
    url: "https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17462v1"
    publishedAt: "2026-11-12"
    type: Preprint
    access: Abstract only
    evidence: >-
      Diffusion-based generative models have become strong priors over plausible
      crystal structures, but turning that prior into promising candidates
      requires steering toward several conflicting properties at once.
      Fine-tuning is expensive and produces a new model for every new objective,
      while differentiable guidance is not applicable to many real-world tasks.
      We instead adapt these generative models at inference time using Diffusion
      Tree Sampling (DTS), a Monte Carlo tree search procedure over the
      denoising trajectory. We make two technical contributions on top of DTS
      that are particularly relevant for AI-for-science applications. First, we
      show how to use existing property-conditional generative models (e.g.,
      models fine-tuned for classifier-free guidance) as guided proposals inside
      DTS, without distorting the target distribution. Second, we extend DTS to
      multi-objective sampling, so that a single search produces samples
      spanning an entire Pareto front. We instantiate both adaptations on de
      novo crystal generation: improving the stability of crystals generated by
      different models, using pretrained conditional models to generate crystals
      belonging to rare space groups or with high bulk modulus, and generating
      crystals along a Pareto front of high dielectric constant and high bandgap
      materials. Across these tasks, DTS-based inference-time adaptation
      successfully improves model outputs without modifying the models
      themselves.

      State-of-the-art atomistic foundation models provide broadly transferable
      energy and force predictions, but their direct deployment in large-scale
      molecular dynamics remains computationally costly. Here we examine whether
      their knowledge can be distilled into compact potentials while retaining
      the physical responses of heterogeneous semiconductor interfaces. From 128
      pretrained models spanning 22 model families, we select a fixed
      EquiformerV3 teacher and train atomic cluster expansion (ACE) and
      neuroevolution potential (NEP) students without target-specific teacher
      fine-tuning, with both simulation speed and accessible system size
      improved by several orders of magnitude relative to the teacher model.
      Matched training configurations and architectures enable direct
      comparisons with students trained on density-functional-theory (DFT)
      reference labels. For 79,920-atom BAs/GaN interfaces, direct distillation
      preserves termination-dependent thermal conductance and dominant spectral
      features, with conductance differing by at most 6.6% from DFT-trained
      counterparts. In 29,792-atom Cu/diamond specimens loaded at 500 K, tensile
      strength and loading work differ by 2.8% and 1.5%, respectively.
      Exploratory SiC/a-C/SiC simulations retain the principal thermal
      bottleneck and the direction of the annealing response. Furthermore, a
      four-model committee retrieves 42-56% of the highest-error teacher
      configurations with a 10% selection budget and improves high-error force
      fitting, but does not consistently improve downstream properties. These
      findings demonstrate that direct distillation can preserve key interfacial
      thermal and mechanical responses in compact interatomic potentials,
      opening a path towards large-scale, high-throughput simulations of
      heterogeneous interfaces.
